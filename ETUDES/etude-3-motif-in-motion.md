# Etude 3: Motif in Motion

Chart: *Minor Swing* (Reinhardt-Grapelli), A minor, the 16-bar A section as written in the iReal chart: Am6 Am6 Dm6 Dm6 / E7 E7 Am6 Am6 / Dm6 Dm6 Am6 Am6 / Bb7 E7 Am6 E7, here played with the chart's alternate chords in bars 9-13 (Dm7, G13b9, Cmaj7, Fmaj7) and the b9 colour on E7 in bar 6. See `SONGS/minor-swing.md`.

Standard tuning `E A D G B e`. 4/4, swung eighth notes: **quarter = 160** to learn it, work up to 180-200. Each bar has eight note slots (1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &) of three columns each, so two-digit frets fit. A slot with no fret is a rest. Chord symbols sit above each bar with the bar number.

## The idea

One idea, moved through the changes. Each chord is treated as a ladder of its own tones (low to high, two octaves), and the same shapes are played on every ladder, so the contour stays the same while the harmony changes under it. Four shapes are used: a rising zig-zag (bars 1, 3, 5, 9, 11), a falling zag (bars 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15), a syncopated shape with rests (bars 7, 13, 16) and an arch (bar 14). The shapes are written as ladder steps, so on a four-note chord the step "two up" is always the next-but-one chord tone. When the chord changes, you hear the same shape in a new colour.

## Practice notes

- Learn the zig-zag on Am6 (bar 1) until it is automatic, then play it on each chord of the form: it is the same finger pattern with different notes.
- The syncopated shape (bars 7, 13, 16) is where the swing lives: play it a little behind the beat.
- Write your own shape as eight numbers from 0 to 7 and run it through the ladders: Am6 = A C E F# A C E F#.

Every pitch in the tab was checked against the chord in its bar by script. Fingerings were chosen to keep the hand moving as little as possible; they are one way to play it, not the only way. Passing notes marked in the idea section are the only notes outside the chord.
