# Etude 3: One Shape, Many Chords

Chart: *Hungaria* (Django Reinhardt), key of G, the 32-bar form from the iReal chart: G6/9 G6/9 Ab6/9 Ab6/9 / G6/9 G6/9 E7 E7 (A), A7 A7 D7 D7 / G/B Bbo7 Am7 D7 (B), the A again, then Am7 Cm6 G/B Bbo7 / Am7 D7 G6/9 G6/9 (C). Ab6/9 is a passing chord a half step above the tonic, and Bbo7 is a dim7 passing chord. See `SONGS/hungaria.md`.

Standard tuning `E A D G B e`. Notes are named in written guitar pitch (the sounding pitch is an octave lower). 4/4, medium up swing, swung eighth notes: **quarter = 140** to learn it, work up to 160-180. Each bar has eight note slots of three columns each, so two-digit frets fit. A slot with no fret is a rest. Chord symbols sit above each bar with the bar number. The line under each row of tab labels every note by its role in that bar's chord: `R` root, `3` or `b3`, `5`, `b5`, `6`, `9`, `b7`, `bb7`, and `a` for a chromatic approach or enclosure note that is not in the chord. A repeated note repeats its label.

Written to the `guitar-etude` fingering rule: while a chord lasts, consecutive notes are at most 3 frets apart and on the same or an adjacent string; at a chord change the line moves at most 6 frets; each bar fits a four-fret window.

## The idea

One idea, moved through the changes. Each chord is treated as a ladder of its own tones (low to high, over two octaves), and the same shapes are played on every ladder, so the contour stays the same while the harmony changes under it. Four shapes are used: a rising zig-zag (bars 1, 3, 5, 9, 11, 15, 17, 19, 21, 25, 29, 31), a falling zag (bars 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16, 18, 20, 22, 24, 26, 30), a syncopated shape with rests (bars 7, 13, 23, 27), an arch (bars 14 and 28), and a closing tag in bar 32. The shapes are written as ladder steps, so on a five-tone chord like G6/9 the step "two up" is the next-but-one chord tone, and on the three-tone G/B the step "three up" returns to the root an octave higher. Style devices: the syncopated shape and the arch each repeat a note (a **double note**), the syncopated shape is built on **pauses**, and the closing tag **encloses** the final G (Ab above, F# below, then G) and rests.

## Practice notes

- Learn the zig-zag on G6/9 (bar 1) until it is automatic, then play it on each chord of the form: it is the same finger pattern with different notes.
- The syncopated shape (bars 7, 13, 23, 27) is where the swing lives: play it a little behind the beat.
- Write your own shape as eight numbers (0 is the lowest chord tone, 5 is the same tone an octave up on a five-tone chord) and run it through the ladders: G6/9 = G A B D E G A B.

Every pitch in the tab was checked against the chord in its bar by script (`.agents/skills/guitar-etude/scripts/check_etude.py`), and so was the fingering rule: inside a chord the biggest jump between notes is 3 frets, at a chord change it is 5 frets, and no bar needs more than a four-fret window. The frets run from 0 to 10. The etude has not been played on an instrument; fingerings are one way to play it, not the only way.
