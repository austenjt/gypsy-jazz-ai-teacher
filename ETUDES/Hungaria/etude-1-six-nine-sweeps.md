# Etude 1: Six-Nine Sweeps

Chart: *Hungaria* (Django Reinhardt), key of G, the 32-bar form from the iReal chart: G6/9 G6/9 Ab6/9 Ab6/9 / G6/9 G6/9 E7 E7 (A), A7 A7 D7 D7 / G/B Bbo7 Am7 D7 (B), the A again, then Am7 Cm6 G/B Bbo7 / Am7 D7 G6/9 G6/9 (C). Ab6/9 is a passing chord a half step above the tonic, and Bbo7 is a dim7 passing chord. See `SONGS/hungaria.md`.

Standard tuning `E A D G B e`. Notes are named in written guitar pitch (the sounding pitch is an octave lower). 4/4, medium up swing, swung eighth notes: **quarter = 140** to learn it, work up to 160-180. Each bar has eight note slots of three columns each, so two-digit frets fit. A slot with no fret is a rest. Chord symbols sit above each bar with the bar number. The line under each row of tab labels every note by its role in that bar's chord: `R` root, `3` or `b3`, `5`, `b5`, `6`, `9`, `b7`, `bb7`, and `a` for a chromatic approach or enclosure note that is not in the chord. A repeated note repeats its label.

Written to the `guitar-etude` fingering rule: while a chord lasts, consecutive notes are at most 3 frets apart and on the same or an adjacent string; at a chord change the line moves at most 6 frets; each bar fits a four-fret window.

## The idea

Arpeggio sweeps on the 6/9 sound that opens the tune, joined by voice leading. Each chord's tones are a ladder (G6/9 is G A B D E), and the bars walk up and down it. The two-bar chords come in pairs: the first bar climbs six chord tones and steps back, the second falls past the root and returns. Ab6/9 gets the same climb and fall as G6/9 (bars 3-4 against bars 1-2), so you hear the shape slide up a half step and back. The single-bar chords alternate an arch (up, then back down) and a valley (down, then back up). The register climbs from the nut end of the neck in bar 1 to the 4th-7th frets in bar 11 (D7) and settles back to the nut end by bar 15. Style devices: a **pause** (an eighth rest) closes bars 4, 8, 12, 13, 20, 24 and 27, just before chord changes; bars 15 and 21 repeat their top note as a **double note**; the Bbo7 bars (14, 28) are plain dim7 arpeggios, because Bbo7 is a chromatic bass note between G/B and Am7 (B, Bb, A). The last bar runs down the G6/9 arpeggio, then **encloses** the root (Ab above, F# below, then G) and rests.

## Practice notes

- Start with bars 1-2, slowly, with strict alternate picking: one bar climbs, the next falls.
- Play bars 1-4 as one phrase and listen to the Ab6/9 shape: it is the G6/9 line a half step up.
- At bars 13-14-15 the bass walks B, Bb, A. Play the three bars slowly and find the nearest chord tone at each change.
- In bar 32, play the enclosure (Ab, F#, G) as three even eighths with the weight on the G, then leave the rest empty.

Every pitch in the tab was checked against the chord in its bar by script (`.agents/skills/guitar-etude/scripts/check_etude.py`), and so was the fingering rule: inside a chord the biggest jump between notes is 3 frets, at a chord change it is 3 frets, and no bar needs more than a four-fret window. The frets run from 0 to 7. The etude has not been played on an instrument; fingerings are one way to play it, not the only way.
