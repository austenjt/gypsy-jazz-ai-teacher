# La Pompe

*La pompe* is the pumping comping rhythm of gypsy jazz. The name is the motion: the right hand keeps moving, the chords are struck on the beat, and beats 2 and 4 are shortened into a percussive choke. The result is a driving, slightly lopsided pulse that leaves room for the bass and pushes the soloist.

Your job is to make the student's comping feel good *before* it looks sophisticated.

## What it should sound like

- Four beats to the bar, all present, none equal.
- Beats 1 and 3: fuller, longer, a little heavier.
- Beats 2 and 4: short, dry, accented — the "choke."
- Continuous motion in the right hand, even where nothing sounds.
- The bottom of the harmony belongs to the bass player. Stay above it.

If the student's comping sounds like a metronome with chords, the accents are missing. If it sounds like a machine gun, the chords are not being released.

## Right-hand mechanics

1. **Wrist, not arm.** The forearm stays quiet; the wrist turns like a door hinge. If the shoulder moves, the tempo will wobble.
2. **Downstrokes on the beat.** The pick travels down through the strings and comes to rest on the next string (rest stroke). The hand returns up during the space between beats.
3. **Release the chord.** The choke comes from the left hand relaxing pressure a fraction after the strike, sometimes helped by the right hand damping with the heel of the palm.
4. **Grip loose enough to drop the pick.** Tension is the most common reason a student cannot get past 160.
5. **Play the strings you need.** Most comping lives on the D, G, B, and high E strings. Low notes blur under a soloist.

Alternative approaches exist — some players use a continuous down-up eighth-note motion with the chord only sounding on the beat, and some players accent differently in a two-feel. Teach one clean version first, then let the student's ear choose.

## Voicing rules for comping

- Prefer 6/9 for major, m6 for minor, 7/9/13 for dominant, dim7 for passing.
- Three or four notes are enough. Five is usually too many at tempo.
- Keep the top voice moving in small steps. Gypsy jazz comping is voice leading, not chord naming.
- Avoid doubling the root in the bass register; the bassist is already there.
- Shapes (fret strings run **low E → high e**, `x` = muted):
  - `x32233` — C6/9
  - `x3223x` — C6/9, thinner
  - `3x233x` — Gm6
  - `x0455x` — Am6
  - `3x243x` — G6
  - `x3233x` — C9
  - `x323xx` — C7 shell

Full tables are in `manouche-harmony/references/voicings.md`.

## Tempo ladder for comping

| Stage | Tempo | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 100–120 | Even 4, clear accents, no rushing |
| 2 | 120–150 | 32 bars without stopping, relaxed wrist |
| 3 | 150–180 | Dynamics: soloist louder than you |
| 4 | 180–220 | No loss of choke; still breathing |
| 5 | 220+ | Clean at the top, one bar in four nearly silent |

Move up only when the previous stage is clean twice in a row.

## Playing behind a soloist

- **Listen for space.** If the soloist plays long notes, comp more; if they play dense lines, comp less.
- **Lower the volume, not the energy.** Keep the attack, reduce the size of the stroke.
- **Follow the shape of the solo.** Build across a chorus rather than resetting every eight bars.
- **Use two-feel.** Half notes or one chord per two bars at the start of a solo, then back to four.
- **Stop-time and breaks** should be agreed, not surprised onto the soloist.
- **Watch the bassist's feet and the soloist's breath**, not your own left hand.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rushing at tempo | Right hand anticipating; arm tension | Metronome on 2 and 4 only; play half the notes |
| Chords ring into each other | No release | Practice silent releases: strike, then mute before the next beat |
| Weak backbeat | Accents played with the wrist instead of the hand | Accent drill at 80 bpm: loud 2 and 4, soft 1 and 3 |
| Muddy sound | Too many low notes; too many notes | Move to the top four strings; drop the fifth |
| Stiff at high tempo | Pick grip, forearm locked | Two minutes of loose eighth notes, then retry |
| Comping fights the soloist | Same register, same density | Play only beats 1 and 3 for a chorus, listen |
| Sounds "straight" | No swing in the eighth-note feel | Play with a recording, not a metronome, for ten minutes |

## Guardrails

- Do not teach la pompe as a strumming pattern alone. It is a time feel; the pattern is the least important part.
- Do not add fills before the four-beat pulse is steady.
- If a student asks about a specific recording, describe what is audible and mark anything uncertain as uncertain.
- Tempos in gypsy jazz are high. Never let a student "practice" at a tempo where the accents disappear.
