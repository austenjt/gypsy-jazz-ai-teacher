# Progressions

Framework first, chords second. If a student knows the Roman numerals, they can play the tune in any key and hear substitutions when they appear.

Chord symbols here are suggestions for comping, not transcriptions of any particular recording. Where a form varies between bands, that is noted.

## Minor swing form

The backbone of the minor-key repertoire. Eight-bar A section, eight-bar B section, AABA.

```
A: | i     | i     | iv    | iv    |
   | i     | i     | V7    | V7    |
B: | iv    | iv    | i     | i     |
   | iv    | iv    | V7    | V7    |
```

In A minor: `Am6 | Am6 | Dm6 | Dm6 | Am6 | Am6 | E7 | E7`, bridge `Dm6 | Dm6 | Am6 | Am6 | Dm6 | Dm6 | E7 | E7`.

Comping versions of the same thing:

- Plain: `Am6` and `Dm6`, `E7` on the dominants.
- With passing chords: insert `Ebdim7` before `Dm6`, `G#dim7` before `Am6`.
- With a tritone sub: `Bb7` instead of `E7` in the last bar of the bridge.
- Minorization: `E7` for two beats, `Em6` for two beats, resolving to `Am6`.

Bands differ on the exact bar count of the bridge in some minor tunes. Confirm the form before a performance; do not assume.

## 12-bar minor blues (A minor)

```
| i     | iv    | i     | i     |
| iv    | iv    | i     | V7    |
| iv    | i     | V7    | i     |
```

In A minor: `Am6 | Dm6 | Am6 | Am6 | Dm6 | Dm6 | Am6 | E7 | Dm6 | Am6 | E7 | Am6`.

Options: `E7#9` on bar 8, `G#dim7` in bar 11, tritone sub `Bb7` in bar 11.

## 12-bar major blues (C)

Quick change in bar 2, the standard jazz blues.

```
| I7    | IV7   | I7    | I7    |
| IV7   | IV7   | I7    | VI7   |
| II7   | V7    | I7 V7 | I7    |
```

In C: `C7 | F7 | C7 | C7 | F7 | F7 | C7 | A7 | D7 | G7 | C7 G7 | C7`.

Options: `C6/9` instead of `C7` on the tonic when the melody allows; `Dm7 G7` in bar 10; `Ab7` (tritone sub) in bar 10.

## Rhythm changes (Bb)

The A section, first eight bars:

```
| I6    | VI7   | ii7   | V7    |
| I6    | VI7   | ii7   | V7    |
```

In Bb: `Bb6 | G7 | Cm7 | F7 | Bb6 | G7 | Cm7 | F7`.

The next four bars usually move through `III7 VI7 II7 V7`; the bridge is a cycle of dominants: `D7 | D7 | G7 | G7 | C7 | C7 | F7 | F7`.

Gypsy-jazz treatment: substitute `Bb6/9` for `Bb6`, keep the dominants plain, and use dim7 passing chords rather than dense extensions.

## Turnarounds

In C:

```
| C6/9  | A7    | Dm7   | G7    |     plain
| C6/9  | A7    | Dm7   | Db7   |     tritone sub on the last chord
| C6/9  | C#dim7| Dm7   | G7    |     diminished passing chord
| C6/9  | A7    | Dm7   | G7#9  |     colored dominant
```

In A minor:

```
| Am6   | F#m7b5| Bm7b5 | E7    |     the minor version
| Am6   | Am6   | Bm7b5 | E7    |     simpler, common
```

## Diminished passing chords

A dim7 resolves because every note sits a half or whole step from a chord tone of the target. Put the dim7 a half step above or below the root you are resolving to, and the voice leading takes care of itself.

- `C6/9 → C#dim7 → Dm7` — half step below the D, resolving up.
- `Am6 → Ebdim7 → Dm6` — half step above the D, resolving down.
- `G7 → G#dim7 → Am6` — the same chord as the dim7 on the b9 of the dominant (b9 of G is Ab, which is G#). One chord, two explanations.

Because dim7 chords are symmetrical, one shape covers four roots a minor third apart. That is why they are the cheapest way to sound sophisticated.

## Intros

- **Turnaround intro (4 bars):** `I – VI7 – II7 – V7`, ending on the V so the head can start.
- **Vamp intro:** two bars of `i – iv` for minor tunes, with the soloist entering on the downbeat.
- **Pedal intro:** hold the dominant in the bass while the upper voices move.
- **Solo guitar intro:** rubato, melody fragments, then the last four bars of the form to set the tempo.
- **Count-in:** four bars of la pompe. Let the tempo settle before the head.

## Endings

- **Turnaround tag:** play the last four bars twice, then land on the tonic 6/9 on beat one and let it ring.
- **Stop-time:** four bars of tonic, one chord per bar, then a final chord on the downbeat.
- **Ritard:** slow the last two bars, and only the last two. Do not slow the whole ending.
- **Dim7 push:** `Imaj6 → I#dim7 → Imaj6`, one beat per chord, as a final gesture.

Announce endings with your eyes and your right hand, not with a hopeful look at the last bar.
