# Transcribe by Ear

Transcription is not a test of ear talent; it is a procedure. Done well, one eight-bar phrase gives more than a month of scale practice, because it gives a player something to *say*.

The goal is never the completed transcription. The goal is the vocabulary that survives into the student's playing.

## The procedure

1. **Choose a short target.** Two to four bars. Not a whole solo.
2. **Listen without the instrument.** Ten times. Sing the phrase badly until the shape is in your head.
3. **Find the rhythm first.** Tap it, sing it on one note. Most transcription errors are rhythm errors.
4. **Find the first note.** Play candidates against the recording until one locks.
5. **Find the contour**, not the notes: up, down, step, leap.
6. **Fill in the notes** one at a time, checking each against the recording.
7. **Write it down** in tab and in note names, with the chord under each bar.
8. **Play it with the recording** at full speed, then slow.
9. **Analyze it**: what device is this — arpeggio, enclosure, dim7, chromatic run, motif?
10. **Transpose it** to three other keys.
11. **Apply it** to one tune, once, deliberately.

Steps 1–8 are the transcription. Steps 9–11 are why it was worth doing.

## Tools

- Slowdown software with pitch preserved (any player that does 50–75% without chipmunking).
- A looper for two-bar chunks. Loop, sing, play, move on.
- Tuner or reference drone to check pitches against the recording's tuning. Old recordings drift.
- Paper or a text file. Write the bar numbers; they keep you honest about the form.

## What to write down

```
Tune: <name>   Recording: <artist, year, if known>   Chorus: <number>
Key: <key>     Tempo: <approx bpm>     Bars: <e.g., 9-12 of the first chorus>

Chords: | Dm6 | Dm6 | G7 | G7 |

Tab (low E -> high e):
e|----------------|
B|----------------|
...

Notes:  A C E ...
Device: descending m6 arpeggio, lands on the 9th of G7
```

If the recording or chorus is not certain, write "unverified". Never invent a citation.

## Turning it into practice

For each transcribed phrase, extract one thing:

| Extract | Exercise |
| --- | --- |
| Rhythm | Play the rhythm on one note over the same changes |
| Shape | Play the contour with different chord tones |
| Device | Isolate the arpeggio or enclosure, run it through all keys |
| Phrasing | Copy the articulation: which notes are long, short, accented |
| Resolution | Practice only the last two notes, the landing |

Ten minutes on the landing is worth an hour on the first eight notes.

## Common mistakes

- **Transcribing too much.** A whole solo becomes a memorized etude nobody uses.
- **Skipping the singing.** If you cannot sing it, you are guessing at notes.
- **Ignoring articulation.** The notes are half the phrase; the attack is the other half.
- **Never transposing.** A phrase in one key is a party trick; in five keys it is vocabulary.
- **Never applying it.** A phrase played only in the practice room is a museum piece.
- **Transcribing from tab.** Tab written by someone else is a different exercise: reading, not listening.

## Choosing what to transcribe

- A phrase the student already hums.
- A phrase at the edge of their technique but not beyond it.
- A phrase that solves a specific problem: a ii–V line, a minor resolution, a fast run.
- Django for vocabulary, later players for vocabulary *and* modernity. Both are useful; do not pretend one is the other.

## Output formats

For a student, produce at most one page:

1. The tab, two to four bars.
2. The chord symbols underneath.
3. Two sentences on the device.
4. One transposition assignment.
5. One "use it here" assignment.

Everything else can wait.

## Guardrails

- Do not claim a transcription is from a specific recording unless the student told you which one, or you are certain.
- If the student's recording is in a different tuning or a half step off, say so before they spend an hour on wrong notes.
- Describe what you hear, not what a fake book says. When the two disagree, the recording wins for transcription and the fake book wins for jam conventions.
- Never present an invented phrase as a quote from a named player.
