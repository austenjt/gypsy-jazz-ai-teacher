# Gypsy Jazz Improvisation

Gypsy jazz solos sound fast because the *right hand* is fast, not because the ideas are complicated. The vocabulary is small: arpeggios, chromatic approaches, diminished runs, and rhythm. What makes it sound like Django's descendants is articulation and phrasing.

Teach in this order: sound, then targets, then vocabulary, then lines.

## Sound first: the rest stroke

- The pick travels **through** the string and comes to rest on the next string. That is what produces the loud, percussive attack.
- Change to a higher-pitched string on a **downstroke**, almost always. Same string: alternate. Lower-pitched string: either, depending on what comes next.
- Wrist drives the pick. The elbow stays close to the body.
- Play one note, loudly, at 60 bpm, for two minutes. Listen for the "thump". If the note sounds thin, the pick is not going through the string.

Players who skip this step hit a tempo ceiling around 160 and never understand why.

## Targets before vocabulary

Improvising over changes is mostly knowing where to land.

1. **Roots only.** One chorus, roots on beat 1 of each chord. Count the bars aloud.
2. **Guide tones.** 3rds and 7ths, one per chord, moving by the smallest interval.
3. **Arpeggios, two notes per chord.** No scales.
4. **Add one passing tone** between targets. Chromatic is fine.
5. **Now add vocabulary** from `references/language.md`.

If the student cannot do step 2, vocabulary will only produce noise.

## The vocabulary stack

Add one device at a time, in this order, and apply each to a real tune before adding the next.

| Device | What it is | Where it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arpeggio run | Chord tones in one direction, usually descending | Any chord |
| Chromatic enclosure | Target approached from above and below | Ends of phrases |
| Triplet burst | Three fast notes into a target on the next beat | Dominants and turnarounds |
| Diminished run | dim7 arpeggio over a dominant, resolving up | V7 → i or V7 → I |
| Repeated notes | Two or three strikes on the same pitch | Building tension, high register |
| Tremolo | Rapid repeated note, wrist-driven | Ballads, dramatic moments |
| Motif | A 3–5 note idea, restated and varied | Whole choruses |

Two devices used well beat six used nervously.

## Building a chorus

```
Bars 1–2    State a motif. Simple, rhythmic, confident.
Bars 3–4    Repeat it, change the ending to fit the next chord.
Bars 5–6    Answer in a different register.
Bars 7–8    Resolve. Land on a chord tone and stop playing.
```

Then a second chorus that starts somewhere else: higher, quieter, or with the motif inverted. A solo needs a shape, not a stream.

## Phrasing rules

- **Leave space.** Two bars of silence in a chorus is a statement.
- **Rhythm carries the line.** If a phrase would survive on one note, it is a good phrase.
- **Land, do not wander.** End phrases on a chord tone, usually the 3rd or the root.
- **Build across the form.** Start conversational, end declarative.
- **Do not outrun the tempo.** At 220, eighth notes are already fast; sixteenths are a special effect.
- **Tone over notes.** One well-placed note beats eight unarticulated ones.

## Practice loop

1. Pick two bars of a tune.
2. Sing a phrase over it. Simple enough to sing is simple enough to play.
3. Find it on the instrument, in one position.
4. Play it at a tempo where it is clean; then up 10 bpm.
5. Transpose it to three other keys.
6. Use it once in a chorus, then stop using it deliberately.

Fretboard grids for dominant, minor 6, and diminished arpeggios, plus device exercises: `references/language.md`.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lines sound like scales | No target tones | Guide-tone chorus, two notes per chord |
| Everything is eighth notes | No rhythmic vocabulary | Phrase with quarter notes and triplets only |
| Fast passages smear | Right hand not resting on the next string | Slow rest-stroke drill, 60 bpm |
| Solos die after four bars | No motif, no plan | Two-bar motif, four variations |
| Sounds generic | Vocabulary not tied to changes | Apply each device to one specific bar of one tune |
| Cannot hear changes | Not listening to the bass | Play roots only behind a recording |

## Guardrails

- Never say "just play the changes" without giving targets.
- Do not invent quotes or attribute lines to recordings you cannot verify.
- Keep tab in fret strings or six-line blocks; state the tuning when in doubt.
- If a student asks for "Django's lick from X", ask which recording and which chorus, and describe what is actually audible.
- Fast is not a goal. Clean at 200 is the goal; 200 with a plan beats 260 without one.
